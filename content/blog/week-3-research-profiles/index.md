This week's task looked bureaucratic: register on academic services and link them from the site. ORCID, Google Scholar, ResearchGate, eLibrary, Academia.edu, Mendeley, arXiv. The icon row under my photo is the visible result.

Registering on research databases as a first-year student feels a bit like printing business cards before having a job. My Google Scholar profile contains zero publications, and its citation graph is a flat line at zero. There's something funny about a service politely offering to notify me when someone cites me.

Still, I see the logic. ORCID gives a researcher a permanent ID, so that all the works of one person stay connected even if they change universities or their name is spelled five different ways in different journals. Getting the ID takes five minutes and costs nothing. If I ever publish anything, the infrastructure is already waiting. If I don't, I lost five minutes.

Some registrations were quick, some weren't. ORCID: instant. eLibrary, the Russian scientific database, has a long form that asks for your organization, department and position, and clearly wasn't written with students in mind. I picked the closest matching options and moved on.

The technical part was pleasant for a change. The template has an icon system, and adding a link is three lines in the author file. Icons for Google Scholar and ORCID come from a special academic icon set, which tells you a lot about the intended audience of this template. Only eLibrary had no icon of its own, so it got a generic book.

A small side effect of the week: I finally understood the difference between arXiv, a preprint server where papers appear before review, and databases like Scholar that index everything. Before, they were all just "science websites" in my head.
