This week the site got its main content: who I am, where I study, what I can do. Sounds like an hour of work. It wasn't.

The template stores all personal data in one YAML file: name, biography, education, skills, links. I like this design. The page layout is someone else's careful work, and I only fill in fields. Break the indentation, though, and the build fails with an error message pointing somewhere completely unrelated. I learned this twice.

Writing a biography at nineteen is a special genre. I have no degrees, no publications and no job title, so every draft either sounded like a joke or like a LinkedIn parody. In the end I wrote three plain sentences about studying applied informatics and keeping notes here. Honest and short beats impressive and fake.

The skills section wants a level from one to five for each entry. Rating yourself is uncomfortable. Python got a four, because I've written enough of it to know what I don't know. C++ got a three, which might be generous. I decided the ratings mean "compared to other first-year students", not "compared to people with jobs", and stopped worrying.

Then the photo. The layout wants a square avatar, my photo was vertical, and every automatic crop either cut my head or kept mostly lawn. Ten minutes of nudging crop boxes for one small circle on a page. Design is effort, even at this scale.

Education was the easy part: school, then RUDN University, applied informatics, started in 2025. One thing I noticed: filling a CV template shows you exactly which sections are still empty. Experience: two modest entries. Publications: none. It reads less like an achievement list and more like a to-do list. That's probably useful.

Next week: registering on research databases and adding proper profile links.
